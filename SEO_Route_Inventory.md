## 🔴 UNBOUNDED ROUTES — infinite URL space

| Route | `generateStaticParams`? | `notFound()`? | Live behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| `industries/[slug]` | **No** | **No** | Any slug → **HTTP 200**. Verified: `/industries/completely-made-up-industry` = 200 |
| `blog/[slug]` | **No** | **No** | Any slug → **HTTP 200**. Verified: `/blog/buy-cheap-viagra-online` = 200, rendering `<h1>Buy Cheap Viagra Online</h1>` |

Both routes title-case the URL slug and inject it directly into the `<h1>` and body copy. There is no allowlist, no data lookup, and no 404 path.

**Consequences:**
1. **Infinite crawl space.** Crawl budget can be consumed without limit; every unique URL returns a 200 with near-identical content.
2. **Open content injection on your own domain.** Any third party can publish a link to `gavior.in/blog/<arbitrary-text>` and Gavior's domain will render that text as the page's H1 with a 200 status. This is the standard mechanism behind parasite-SEO and spam-link campaigns, and it is live right now.
3. **Scaled content abuse exposure.** Google's spam policies cover mass-generated pages with little added value. Infinite templated pages generated from URL strings is a textbook match.
4. `/blog` currently links to at least one such article, so the trap is discoverable by crawlers, not merely reachable.

**This is the highest-severity finding in the audit and it outranks every metadata issue.**
