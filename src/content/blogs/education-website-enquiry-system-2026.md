An education website is often asked to do too many jobs at once: introduce the institution, explain programmes, answer family questions, collect enquiries and support admissions teams. The answer is not more pages. It is a clearer path from first interest to a useful next action.

**Disclosure:** Gavior builds websites, CRM workflows and growth systems. This article is designed to help education teams scope a practical project.

## Start with the prospective student’s decision

Most prospective students or families are trying to answer a short set of questions:

- Is this programme relevant to my goal?
- What will I learn, how long will it take and what does it cost?
- What are the entry requirements and important dates?
- Can I speak to someone or attend an open day?
- What is the next step if I am ready to apply?

Give every programme page a visible answer to those questions. Avoid hiding critical information in PDFs or requiring an enquiry just to understand the offer. An enquiry form works best after the visitor has enough confidence to ask a specific question.

## Build pages around programmes, not internal departments

An admissions journey typically needs a small, consistent content model:

| Page element | What it should clarify |
| --- | --- |
| Programme summary | Who it is for and the outcome it supports. |
| Curriculum or experience | What the learner will do, not only module names. |
| Entry and cost information | The criteria and investment required to make a decision. |
| Proof | Relevant outcomes, faculty context, student work or facilities. |
| Next step | Apply, attend an event, request a call or download a prospectus. |

Consistency allows an internal team to update content without redesigning every course page. It also makes campaign landing pages easier to create when admission periods change.

## Route enquiries with context

“Name, email and phone number” is rarely enough for an admissions adviser to respond well. Capture the programme or campus of interest, intended start period and the question the person wants answered. Pass that information to the team’s CRM or admissions inbox with a clear owner and response expectation.

The useful metric is not form volume alone. Track qualified enquiries, response time, booked conversations, started applications and completed enrolments. That shows where the experience or follow-up process needs attention.

## Make mobile performance a conversion requirement

Families often research on a phone before comparing details on a larger screen. Keep programme pages fast, readable and easy to act on. Google defines Core Web Vitals around loading, responsiveness and visual stability, and recommends measuring them with real-user data where possible. See [Google’s Core Web Vitals guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals).

This is not an argument for designing to a score alone. It is a reminder to protect the experience when campaign traffic arrives on slower devices and networks.

## A focused first release

For many institutions, the first useful release is:

1. A structured programme template.
2. Clear admissions and event calls to action.
3. A contextual enquiry form connected to the correct team.
4. Analytics for each important next step.
5. An editor-friendly way to update dates, fees and programme details.
