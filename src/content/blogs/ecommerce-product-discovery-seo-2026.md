An e-commerce site can have attractive product photography and still lose a sale if shoppers cannot find the right item, trust the details or complete checkout without friction. Product discovery is a connected system: taxonomy, navigation, product data, performance, search visibility and fulfilment clarity all matter.

**Disclosure:** Gavior builds commerce sites and search-led growth systems. This article is a technical and commercial planning guide, not a promise of search rankings or revenue.

## Make category navigation useful to people and crawlers

Start with the language customers use to browse. Categories should reflect real buying decisions—product type, use case, collection or compatible range—not internal stock codes alone. Give each item a reachable path from a category or collection page.

Google recommends a crawler-friendly navigation structure with links from menus to categories, sub-categories and product pages. Its [e-commerce site-structure guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure?hl=en) also explains why links and structured data help search engines understand what matters on the site.

## Give product pages enough decision information

Every product page needs a clear answer to:

- What is it and who is it for?
- What is included, available and compatible?
- What will it cost, including relevant delivery context?
- How can the customer return, exchange or get help?
- Why should someone trust this product or this store?

Do not reuse a supplier description unchanged across the entire catalogue. Write enough original, accurate information to make a decision possible. Use images, specifications and reviews only where they are genuine and maintained.

## Keep product data consistent

Price, availability, variants, shipping and return policies need an owner. If the storefront, inventory system and marketing feed disagree, the customer experience becomes unreliable.

Google’s [product structured-data documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product) explains how machine-readable product data can make a page eligible for richer search appearances. Eligibility is not a guarantee of a specific result, but accurate structured data helps search engines understand product information.

## Protect performance on high-intent pages

Treat product and checkout pages as performance priorities. Optimise media delivery, reserve space for images, limit non-essential scripts and test filters, carts and variant selectors on mobile networks. Google’s Core Web Vitals guidance uses loading, responsiveness and visual stability to describe real-world experience signals.

## Measure discovery through to completed orders

Look beyond total sessions. Track category-to-product progression, search usage, out-of-stock encounters, add-to-cart rate, checkout error recovery and completed purchase rate. Segment by device and source. The goal is to find the point where purchase intent turns into avoidable work.
